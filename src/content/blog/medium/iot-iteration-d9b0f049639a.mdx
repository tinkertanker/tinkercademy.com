<ArticleLegacyBlock as="h3" html={"An introduction to one of our most intricate workshops for corporate techies"} />

<ArticleLegacyBlock as="p" html={"One of the most fun workshops we conduct is a tech competition, aimed at folks who already have some programming or technology background. The theme and scope of the competition may vary, but participants are always divided into groups, to compete against one another to solve technological challenges the fastest."} />

<ArticleLegacyBlock as="p" html={"Earlier this year, we started devising this particular workshop. The specifications were a bit daunting— 2 hours, 120+ participants, no hardware on site (meaning we needed to bring computers, monitors, peripherals), with a Rube-Goldberg theme that would culminate with having participants build and fire off a DIY wooden &amp; PVC catapult. We tapped on our library of toys and curriculum, to include topics such as cryptography, robotics, IoT data collection, electronics, logic gates, API usage, Raspberry Pi, and that aforementioned catapult (which had to be remotely triggered)."} />

<ArticleLegacyBlock as="p" html={"The result: due to hardware limitations (we just didn’t have enough monitors for the coding challenges), the groups weren’t always able to tackle the challenges in parallel. Another problem was how the groups were supposed to each use a <a href=\"https://shop.littlebits.cc/products/cloudbit\">littleBits cloudbit</a>, but they had no way to identify which cloudbit wifi was their own without them being labelled prior. Thankfully, a lot of the participants had a great time, but, oh boy, that was a logistics nightmare."} />

<ArticleLegacyBlock as="h2" html={"<strong>Back to the Drawing Board</strong>"} />

<ArticleLegacyBlock as="p" html={"One of the participants from that previous challenge saw the potential of the workshop, and asked us to develop a full-day competition around a similar theme. Looking back at it, despite the numerous issues that cropped up, we had managed to come up with a design that participants found engaging and unique, and which really tapped on their innate competitive drive."} />

<ArticleLegacyBlock as="p" html={"Come this new workshop, the specs were a bit different: 8 teams, 60+ people, and 7 hours of classroom time. And this time, we aimed to combine even more things in our <a href=\"http://gethacking.com\">arsenal of gadgets and toys</a>: littleBits, micro:bit, Strawbees, Python/R data analysis, APIs, IFTTT, with an overarching theme of IoT. Simple, right?"} />

<ArticleLegacyBlock as="p" html={"Learning from that previous experience was incredibly important in designing this new IoT challenge. With a few tweaks on version 1.0, we thought we could deliver a really engaging competition. Most importantly, we added a 2.5 hour briefing period at the start of the day, where we could introduce them to the hardware and software that they’d be using."} />

<ArticleLegacyBlock as="p" html={"<em>The calm before the storm!</em>"} className="medium-image-caption" />

<ArticleLegacyBlock as="p" html={"Participants would still need to go beyond the basic intro that we provided them, but they’d have basic knowledge and a starting point so they didn’t end up flailing about in the dark. Second, trying to supply hardware to so many people was a big issue during the first iteration, so instead, we required them to bring laptops and hosted a <a href=\"http://jupyter.org\">Jupyter</a> server for them to complete data analysis challenges rather than using the Raspberry Pi and all the peripherals. No more need to worry about transporting monitors, keyboards, and mice! Likewise, we (sadly) removed the catapult challenge. While it was really, really awesome (thanks to our intern Shao Hong for creating it!), it was just too difficult to bring down hardware supplies and tools for so many people."} />

<ArticleLegacyBlock as="p" html={"Finally, we needed to add some challenges to account for the longer competition length. We had been doing a <a href=\"https://tinkercademy.com/blog/2017/getting-singapore-making-with-the-micro-bit/\">lot of work with the micro:bit</a> as it’s currently being introduced to the Singapore schools, so we added in a challenge to build a micro:bit bluetooth RC car to simulate an IoT data collection device. Likewise, cryptography is an important aspect of IoT security, so one of our interns (thanks Wei Heng!) was able to develop three devious crypto challenges. Finally, we had a very challenging littleBits hardware challenge to add as well."} />

<ArticleLegacyBlock as="p" html={"Also, two of our interns (thanks Sam &amp; Si Jie!) had previously designed a server to display a real-time scoreboard. And this was the perfect time to dust it off. Thankfully, Si Jie was free to recover and adapt the code <em>(…at 1 am the night before…sorry!</em>)."} />

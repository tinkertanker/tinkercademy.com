<h2>Re-interning at Tinkertanker</h2>

<p><em>Editor’s note: Mireille is currently finishing her final year at Raffles Institution. She interned with us for 8 weeks at the end of 2020, and managed to such a lot done in that time! She made things, fixed things, automated things, coded things— and even helped us </em>sell<em> things, when walk-in customers showed up. We loved her energy and drive, and we’re happy for her to share her story here.</em></p>

<h2>Introduction</h2>

<p>Last December (2019), I had completed a 2-week work experience at Tinkertanker, having heard about the company from my former Robotics coach. Looking back, it felt longer than a mere fortnight; I was able to do many things that I loved and meet so many amazing people.</p>

<p>For that reason, after my Y5 Promotional Examinations, as I was wondering whether to take up another internship, the company came to mind almost immediately. As such, I contacted Tinkertanker once again, and immediately upon the end of school classes, embarked on my 1.5-month-long internship.</p>

<h2>What I Did Last Year</h2>

<p>My first work experience was primarily focused on assisting with the creation of the Twelve Days of Christmas tutorials — by writing the tutorial drafts, helping out with the photoshoots, or both.</p>

<h2>Why I Returned</h2>

<p>Regarding the idea of an internship, simply put, I just wanted to spend my school holidays being productive and doing stuff that I would not get the chance to do normally. It was definitely better than the alternative of staying at home all day every day (I had enough of that, thanks to Circuit Breaker).</p>

<p>But why Tinkertanker? There are a couple of reasons:</p>

<ol>
<li>My attachment last year felt too short and I wanted to do more.</li>
<li>I kind of missed the office environment and the people there.</li>
<li>Interning at Tinkertanker would have allowed me to pursue my interests in design and technology, and the company specialising in education is an added bonus for me.</li>
</ol>

<p>And so, I contacted the company…</p>

<h2>What I Have Done This Year</h2>

<p>To summarise, this year’s internship comprised of assisting for events and R&amp;D. Of course, there was a lot of fabrication (i.e. laser-cutting, 3D printing, etc.) as well! Without further ado, here is a summary of the past one-and-a-half months.</p>

<h2><strong>DigiGirlz 2020 and Power Automate</strong></h2>

<p>I joined the company on the week of DigiGirlz, a two-day programme by Microsoft consisting of training on Power Apps as well as a hackathon.</p>

<!-- medium-image:0*vd43u_OW5J0TGcw4 alt-decision:meaningful -->

<img src="/blog-media/98ca18311f308d54c4214fec97313c9da65876ae9d716c3b34ba69da63cfa123.jpg" alt="DigiGirlz 2020 banner" width="1560" height="675" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

<p>The event had a stunning 500+ participants and a lot of admin work to handle, so I spent the first few days of my internship doing “Spreadsheet Fu”, which is like Kung Fu, but with well… spreadsheets. For the event itself, I was a facilitator as well as a “camera man” for the live event. During the event itself, I got introduced to Power Apps, which one can think of as app development with Excel functions.</p>

<p>I think the true value behind the event (for me at least), however, came after it. In the spirit of the event, I managed to create a certificate generator that will automatically create and mail certificates using Power Automate.</p>

<h2>Research &amp; Development (R&amp;D)</h2>

<p>A good bulk of my internship was spent researching and developing what could potentially be new curriculum! I will just briefly describe each one, lest this post get too long.</p>

<h3><strong>micro:bit v2 X AI</strong></h3>

<p>With the new micro:bit v2.0 comes a microphone and amazing new capabilities! A while back, Edge Impulse uploaded an <a href="https://www.edgeimpulse.com/blog/voice-activated-microbit">article</a> on combining the micro:bit with Machine Learning so that it becomes voice-activated. All this time, I saw the micro:bit as a very basic microcontroller capable of simple input and output and nothing more. Consequently, the potential of this project excited me, so when given the option, I decided to take it on!</p>

<p>The model was trained using <a href="https://www.edgeimpulse.com/">Edge Impulse</a>, which I find works really well; it is no-code so it can be easily used to teach those with no prior experience when it comes to programming or machine learning!</p>

<blockquote>Edge Impulse enables developers to create the next generation of intelligent device solutions with embedded machine learning.</blockquote>

<aside class="article-link-card"><a href="https://photos.app.goo.gl/Vri1vQZTn4WSjGDR9"><strong>micro:bit X AI Part 2 by Mireille Tan</strong></a><a href="https://photos.app.goo.gl/Vri1vQZTn4WSjGDR9"><br /></a><a href="https://photos.app.goo.gl/Vri1vQZTn4WSjGDR9"><em>Here is a demo of using the voice-activated micro:bit to turn a servo motor. </em></a><a href="https://photos.app.goo.gl/Vri1vQZTn4WSjGDR9">photos.app.goo.gl</a></aside>

<p>I made a couple of extensions stemming from the original project, including making a servo turn, and having more than one keyword to make a LED control system.</p>

<p>From this project, I had also learnt how to use FFmpeg to reduce the sample frequency of downloaded audio so that I did not have to record my voice <em>too</em> many times to get data for training the ML model.</p>

<h3>micro:bit X Alexa</h3>

<p>Since I already had a micro:bit on hand from the previous R&amp;D project, I decided to give this a try. Unfortunately, it proved to be more difficult than I first thought, mainly because there were too many libraries that could not be downloaded. Lesson learnt: Linux is great for libraries.</p>

<p>Fortunately, a workaround I found was to use <a href="https://www.voiceflow.com/">Voiceflow</a>.</p>

<blockquote>Voiceflow allows individuals &amp; businesses to easily design, build, and launch voice apps without coding.</blockquote>

<p>Using Voiceflow, I managed to make a custom Alexa skill integrated with Google Sheets. This means that the device can poll the Google Sheets and communicate with the micro:bit separately, making for an indirect connection between Alexa and the micro:bit!</p>

<h2>Conclusion</h2>

<p>Whilst my second time at the company was longer than my first, it was honestly short compared to those of all the other interns. That said, my one regret is not having had enough time to dive into deeper coding content (since a lot of my R&amp;D and projects ended up being no-code).</p>

<p>Nevertheless, this internship felt a lot more productive than last year’s. The above post just scratches the surface of all the designing and debugging that I did. I got to try so many different projects ranging from fabrication to machine learning! <strong>I believe this is one of the things that makes an internship at Tinkertanker so special — no matter what your niche is, as long as you love tinkering, you will be able to do something that you enjoy. It may not be something you are familiar with (like me and machine learning), but that just allows you to step out of your comfort zone in an environment that is full of support and optimised for learning. You will likely grow to love the things you do, and only further boost your passion. Every day in Tinkertanker is a chance to learn something new.</strong></p>

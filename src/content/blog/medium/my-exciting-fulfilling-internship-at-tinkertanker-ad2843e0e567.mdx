![Introduction](/blog-media/e50eaa6f3e5f86bd104805d61056e950a4db8d0b40eeef52ded60fde61cb0f8d.png)

In December, I was looking for an internship after taking a break following the A-levels. I was asking around and searching on various online internship portals for an exciting internship that would allow me to challenge myself, and explore my interests in both the fields of technology and business.

My friend, who was part of a JC programme called INVENT, suggested that I try out for an internship at Tinkertanker. The company had previously taught me for my secondary school’s Infocomm Club Front-end Web Development lessons (HTML, CSS, Javascript), and coached me for a game development competition back in secondary school. Having personally benefited from their interactive curriculum, I decided to take my friend’s suggestion and apply for an internship.

{/* medium-image:1*7hl-gLGjg2KJVGLldsnoEA.png alt-decision:meaningful */}

![Some of my Tinkercademy JavaScript class notes way back in secondary school 😂](/blog-media/ffefe3079af0af60e845d84ec54090f7137df8f4168ecec9d307a7161ddba977.png)

<em>Some of my Tinkercademy JavaScript class notes way back in secondary school 😂</em>

Right from the get-go, Tinkertanker’s internship opportunity stood out to me. Mr Soon told me about the different ways where I could be involved — from hardware, making, coding projects in a wide variety of programming languages, to teaching, marketing and social media content creation across its various business units. There were so many avenues where I could contribute and play a pivotal role in business operations, unlike many other internship opportunities, that seemed to largely revolve around routine administrative tasks.

Thankfully, I was really fortunate to be offered an internship position, and I’ve not looked back since. It turned out to be exactly what I was looking for, and so much more! I had the opportunity to be a part of <a href="http://tinkercademy.com">Tinkercademy</a> and <a href="http://lets.gethacking.com">Let’s Get Hacking</a> (a tutorial site and movement for makers) too, which allowed me to experience so many different and exciting things that I wouldn’t have otherwise — hardware, software, curriculum, teaching, content creation, marketing, and much more!

I am very grateful for the opportunity to intern at this vibrant company. With a wide variety of business operations and arms (<a href="http://tinkercademy.com">Tinkercademy</a>, <a href="http://gethacking.com">Get Hacking</a> online store, <a href="http://guestday.com">GuestDay</a> iPad guest registration, <a href="http://arclab.io">ArcLab</a> mobile training SaaS platform, <a href="http://lets.gethacking.com">Let’s Get Hacking</a> tutorial site), there’s really never a dull moment at Tinkertanker.

## Python for Business

Not long after I joined, I had the opportunity to help out at a 2-day Python for Business course for accountants. This course was primarily taught in Jupyter notebooks and is a relatively intensive one, aimed at familiarising participants with Python — all the way from basic Python and programming concepts, to dataframes, Python libraries like matplotlib and plot.ly for data visualisation, and tweepy for accessing the Twitter API.

## Curriculum Development for MicroPython Classes

My experience developing some slides and answers for the IBM P-tech class also provided me with valuable experience in curriculum development, and I was really grateful when I was offered the opportunity by Mr Soon to create curriculum slides for a series of MicroPython lessons, based on a scheme of work. Aimed at introducing and familiarising Python to students who’ve had some block-based programming experience, the curriculum included not just basic Python concepts and exercises, but also made use of the BBC micro:bit hardware in some lessons, as an interactive medium to ease this transition (MicroPython is basically Python code for the micro:bit hardware!). This allowed me to gain new experiences with coding for hardware. Exercises included creating a mood logger, random dice and magic 8-ball on the micro:bit, and even controlling a ring:bit car powered by the micro:bit!

A really interesting experience for me would definitely be the <a href="https://gethacking.com/products/micro-bit-ring-bit-car-v2">ring:bit car</a>. As opposed to coding the car on MakeCode where we can easily import the “ringbitcar” extension and drag out the relevant movement control blocks, there isn’t a Python library for the ring:bit car, which meant that the servos for the wheels have to be manually coded and various movement functions have to be manually defined. This required knowledge of coding for hardware, such as setting the hertz and pulse cycles for sending signals to the servo wheels of the car.

Other than the above-mentioned Python classes and curriculum, I also wrote documentation for various micro:bit components as a guide for another school. ## Product Review

My involvement in the marketing efforts has been a whole bunch of new but exciting experiences, which I was not exposed to previously. Working with the <a href="https://gethacking.com/products/pirate-radio-pi-zero-w-project-kit?_pos=1&amp;_sid=531e0fc4f&amp;_ss=r">Pirate Radio Kit</a>, a product sold on the company’s online Get Hacking store, and writing a product review for it, was no exception too. Here, I stepped out of my comfort zone and got my hands on this Raspberry Pi Zero W project that involved soldering. ## Laser Cutting

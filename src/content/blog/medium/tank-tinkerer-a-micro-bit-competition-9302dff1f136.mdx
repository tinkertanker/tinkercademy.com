A year ago, we wrote about <a href="https://blog.tinkercademy.com/digital-maker-programme-998927090ddc">Getting Singapore making with micro:bit and the Digital Maker Programme</a>. Since then, we’ve been busy creating <a href="https://gethacking.com/collections/microbit/">new micro:bit kits</a> and writing <a href="http://tinkercademy.com/microbit">tutorials for our micro:bit Tinker Kit</a>— and we’ll tell you more about these soon!—but here’s an exciting project we’ve been working on: designing our very own micro:bit competition.

For this year’s National Infocomm Challenge, we wanted to do something different from the “usual” micro:bit hackathon. So, for the inaugural version of this micro:bit competition in Singapore, participants will create their own game controllers to play a capture-the-flag style video game we made, called <strong>Tank Tinkerer</strong>:

<ArticleEmbed provider="youtube" href={"https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x-Dd8rvdGbM"} title={"Tank Tinkerer: Digital Maker Infocomm Challenge 2018"} />

(And yes, it’s called Tank Tinkerer because that’s an anagram of our <a href="http://tinkertanker.com">company name</a>. We felt clever for <em>days</em> after coming up with this.)

This is the official competition website, just for info, because registration has closed and we’re on to the finals soon:

> <a href="http://tinkercademy.com/tanktinkerer">Digital Making National Infocomm Challenge - Tinkercademy</a>

At time of writing, we’ve reviewed 60+ teams in the preliminaries, and are looking forward to the finals at <a href="http://techsaturday.sg">Tech Saturday this weekend</a> (2 June, 10am) at Suntec City. We’ll share pictures and videos on our social media (<a href="http://twitter.com/tinkercademy">Twitter</a>, <a href="http://facebook.com/tinkercademy">Facebook</a>, <a href="http://instagram.com/tinkercademy">Instagram</a>), and we’ll hopefully have our very talented young intern Jerrayl write about his experience creating the game in Unity.

In the meantime, we’d like to share a bit more about the competition: what is Tank Tinkerer, how is Tank Tinkerer played, and (with apologies to one green-skinned Guardian), <em>why</em> is Tank Tinkerer.

## What is Tank Tinkerer?

The game itself is available for download from itch.io, linked below.

> <a href="https://tinkercademy.itch.io/tanktinkerer">Tank Tinkerer by tinkercademy</a>

<strong>What do participants actually </strong><strong><em>do</em></strong><strong> in this competition?</strong> The simple answer: Design micro:bit controllers that send wireless signals to control their tanks in the Tank Tinkerer game.

What we <em>hope</em> participants do, though, is to make the controllers be as <em>visually striking</em> as possible. As such, we’re hoping for entries such as giant cardboard constructions, DIY craft props, a co-ordinated dance sequence, and/or lots and lots of wild, flailing limb movements.

## How does Tank Tinkerer work?

We (well… mostly Jerrayl) made the game in Unity, out of a variety of open-source models and prefabs—you can grab a copy of the source code here:

> <a href="http://github.com/Tinkertanker/tanktinkerer">Tinkertanker/TankTinkerer</a>

<em><strong>Aside:</strong></em><em> Look at that URL! Tinkertanker/TankTinkerer! Ahha haaha haha! Oh, ahem, sorry.</em>

<strong>How do controllers talk to the game?</strong> The Tank Tinkerer game reads serial input from a <em>receiver </em>micro:bit, connected to the game computer by USB. The micro:bit controllers, or <em>senders</em>, simply send keyboard commands (e.g. W to move forward, S to move back) to the received to control the game. Our <a href="https://docs.google.com/document/d/1cwugjOTJTUyKDmO4TNX1o4s0DKTSO2RnFVEE9vkV93M/">installation guide</a> has information anyone needs to get the game set up properly.

<strong>Won’t participants experience lots of lag time by having to send their controls over the micro:bit radio? </strong>Yes. This is intentional, and also hilarious to watch.

<strong>How do participants win? </strong>We split the competition into two main categories, with prizes for the top controller designs, and a 64-team tournament for the best players. We’re also hoping to see some teams dress up for the Best Theme.

## Why this competition?

When designing this competition, we were heavily inspired by the Nintendo LABO launch video from earlier this year:

<ArticleEmbed provider="youtube" href={"https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P3Bd3HUMkyU"} title={"First Look at Nintendo Labo"} />

By having the competition be about designing game controllers, we aim to have participants create physical, real-world objects that interact with our game’s virtual world. We think this is a great way to bring together maker concepts such as sensors &amp; actuators, inter-device connectivity, and even a dash of augmented reality, to excite participants into thinking up their next great connected device project.
